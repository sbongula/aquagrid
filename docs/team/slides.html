RishikThe problem

Water and energy are the
same problem

Fresh water comes from rain when it falls, and from a desalination plant when it does not.

That plant is the largest electrical load on the island.

Its power is solar when the sun is up, diesel when it is not.

And the diesel arrives by barge, every few weeks — whether or not you are running low.

RishikThe problem

Almost nowhere is it made

It is delegated to a wall timer, usually set to overnight hours inherited from mainland off-peak electricity tariffs.

On a solar island that is exactly backwards. Those are the hours with no sun.

Tuvalu makes it concrete. The freshwater lens is thin and salt-contaminated, so the island runs on rain. In 2011 a drought left Funafuti with days of water and a declared state of emergency. The plant was not too small — nobody could see far enough ahead to act early.

SrihanThe architecture

No backend, on purpose

An app that needs a server to decide whether to run a pump fails exactly when an island is cut off — which is exactly when it matters.

Two AI systems. Only one decides.

RandomForest

Trained by us. Makes every decision — every pump hour, every alert.

GPT-OSS 120B

Open-weight, optional. Makes none. Remove it and every number is unchanged.
